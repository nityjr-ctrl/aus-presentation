## The clinical distinctions to know before rehearsing

- **SUI:** leakage with increased abdominal pressure due to inadequate outlet closure. An AUS addresses this mechanism.
- **OAB versus DO:** OAB is a symptom syndrome centred on urgency; DO is an involuntary detrusor contraction observed during filling cystometry. Either can occur without the other.
- **DU:** insufficient contraction strength and/or duration to empty normally. Low flow alone cannot distinguish DU from obstruction. BCI = Pdet at Qmax + 5 x Qmax; a research threshold is not an AUS eligibility rule.
- **Compliance:** change in bladder volume divided by change in detrusor pressure, in mL/cmH2O. Poor compliance produces a sustained pressure rise during filling. Check subtraction, leakage and artefact before interpreting it.
- **BNC versus VUAS:** bladder-neck contracture usually follows surgery with prostate tissue remaining; VUAS is narrowing of the bladder-to-urethra anastomosis after radical prostatectomy.
- **Continence versus survival:** zero pads, one safety pad, pad-weight reduction, patient satisfaction and freedom from further surgery are different endpoints.
- **Non-inferiority:** excludes a prespecified unacceptable loss of benefit on one endpoint. It does not demonstrate identical results or equivalence in every subgroup.
- **Observational association:** can inform risk counselling, but cannot establish that changing the associated factor prevents the outcome. Confounding by indication is especially important when surgeons choose salvage techniques for the worst urethras.

## Slide 2 | The decision before theatre

### Clinical explanation

Severe leakage establishes burden, but does not establish that AUS implantation will deliver the patient's goal. You need demonstrated SUI, acceptable storage, reliable emptying and the ability to operate the pump. A technically functioning implant may leave the patient restricted by urgency or recurrent retention.

The four options are a useful clinical framework. Implant when the mechanism and functional conditions align. Optimise a treatable dominant problem, such as bothersome urgency. Investigate an uncertain mechanism, trace or outlet. Reconsider the endpoint when safe storage, drainage or usable pump function is unlikely. These options can change after new information.

### Evidence meaning

## Slide 4 | Leakage mechanism and patient goals

### Clinical explanation

Ask what happens during an ordinary active day. Coughing, standing and walking leakage suggest SUI; urgency preceding leakage suggests a storage component. Night-time leakage and difficult voiding need explanation. Demonstrate stress leakage with a suitably filled bladder, rather than accepting a dry examination immediately after voiding.

Record severity in context. Six lightly used pads differ from six saturated pads. A measured pad weight quantifies leakage but depends on collection conditions and activity. A bladder diary can reveal true frequency, small voids or precautionary voiding to avoid stress leakage. Pair a validated symptom/quality-of-life measure with a patient goal, such as walking without soaked clothing.

### Evidence meaning

BAUS25, EAU26 and AUA24 support structured assessment and patient-centred counselling. They do not make pad number an independent eligibility test. The slide combines symptoms, objective demonstration, severity and desired benefit as clinical reasoning.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**PAD26:** A retrospective analysis of prospectively collected follow-up included 2,040 men and 8,787 paired measurements. Pad count tracked leakage well overall (Spearman rho 0.94), but two-to-four pads/day had considerable weight variability. Repeated observations are not independent additional patients; single-centre first-year post-RP follow-up is not a universal AUS selection rule. Pad weight is particularly helpful when the intermediate count, activity or pad type leaves severity uncertain.

### A speaking line

"I want to know what type of leakage occurs, how much it restricts him, and what improvement would make the implant worthwhile."

### Be ready for

The AUS addresses outlet weakness. Counsel a man with mixed symptoms that urgency may remain even when activity-related leakage improves.

## Slide 5 | Timing after prostate treatment

### Clinical explanation

Balance continued recovery against prolonged avoidable disability. Establish whether pelvic-floor training was properly delivered and whether leakage is improving. BAUS's recovery periods differ by severity: at least six months for severe SUI and twelve months for mild-to-moderate SUI. AUA permits surgery from six months for bothersome, non-improving leakage despite conservative therapy, and recommends offering surgery at one year when it persists.

A sling can suit selected men with less severe leakage and favourable tissue. Greater severity, irradiation and complex outlet history alter that conversation. Explain the alternatives, including containment and continued conservative care, without treating them as failure to offer surgery.

### Evidence meaning

BAUS25 and AUA24 provide source-specific timing recommendations; EAU26 supplies the wider treatment context. MASTER22 randomised 380 men to sling or AUS and met its non-inferiority criterion on its primary endpoint. It excluded prior continence implants and unresolved narrowing. This does not make sling and AUS equivalent for irradiated complex revisions, nor establish timing after prostatectomy.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**MASTER26:** This is extended follow-up of the same 380 randomised men, not a second independent trial. At 24 months, sling met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion on the strict self-reported continence endpoint: risk difference -0.006 (95% CI -0.092 to 0.080), or -0.6 percentage points (-9.2 to +8.0). Further surgery was reported in 20 men (11%) after sling and four (2%) after AUS. Secondary outcomes generally favoured AUS. The 15-percentage-point margin applies to the prespecified endpoint, not every outcome. Do not translate non-inferiority into identical effectiveness, zero-pad equivalence or an endorsement for irradiated/reconstructed revision cases. Abstract symptom-score means and adjusted difference are distinct estimates; do not subtract them and label it the adjusted effect.

### A speaking line

"Timing follows severity and recovery trajectory. The sling trial informs selected primary cases; it does not remove the importance of tissue quality and prior treatment."

### Be ready for

Non-inferiority depends on the chosen endpoint and margin. Several MASTER secondary outcomes favoured AUS; both treatments improved symptoms.

## Slide 6 | Pump use over time

### Clinical explanation

Observe the patient operating a demonstration pump. Arthritis, neuropathy, tremor and weak pinch may emerge during the task despite reassuring answers about hand function. Check reach while seated and discuss the intended hemiscrotum. Obesity, restricted hips, scrotal anatomy or another device can make access difficult.

Assessment includes understanding cycling, deactivation and the need to alert clinicians before catheterisation. Consider progressive neurological disease, cognition and frailty over the device's lifetime. Support from relatives may help, but does not necessarily provide timely operation every time the patient needs to void.

### Evidence meaning

AUA24 and ICS16 require adequate physical and cognitive capability; CCIFU describes the dexterity requirement and operating precautions. Demonstration, seated reach and prospective frailty assessment are practical implementations. These citations do not validate a universal dexterity score or an age cutoff. The schematic image illustrates pump location, not proven patient accessibility.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**FRAIL26:** In 7,252 men undergoing AUS or sling, claims-defined frailty was associated with 30-day complications independently of age and comorbidity. Relative to not frail, adjusted relative risks were 1.5 for prefrail and 2.5 for mildly-to-severely frail men. Revision/removal was also associated with frailty. The data mix AUS and sling procedures, come from historical Medicare records and rely on a claims frailty index. These relative risks are not an individual AUS-specific absolute probability, and they do not validate a bedside pump score. Use a practical frailty assessment alongside observed dexterity and cognition.

### A speaking line

"Pump suitability is a demonstrated task, including access and understanding, and I want to consider how that task may change with future frailty."

### Be ready for

If he cannot operate the device independently, reconsider the treatment or seek specialist planning; implantation alone cannot solve that functional limitation.

## Slide 9 | Interpreting an imperfect study

### Clinical explanation

A leaking bladder may never experience a meaningful filling challenge. A low recorded capacity can reflect leakage, urgency, discomfort, filling conditions or genuine storage dysfunction. Review the actual traces, filling stop reason, pressure subtraction and accounted volumes, then compare with diary volumes.

An intubated pressure-flow study can inhibit voiding or mask stress leakage. Conversely, abdominal voiding through an incompetent outlet may not predict emptying after continence surgery. Repeat representative free flow and residual measurements where necessary. If an unreliable finding determines safety, resolve it before implanting.

### Evidence meaning

BAUS25, AUA24 and ICS16 supply assessment principles. UDS14 examined men who failed to leak during intubated testing: 32/169 did not demonstrate leakage until the urethral catheter was removed. This supports recognising a false-negative laboratory result; it does not justify discarding all non-demonstrating tests or omitting the wider assessment.

### A speaking line

"The trace must make sense beside his usual voiding and leakage. A printed number from a poor study cannot settle candidacy."

### Be ready for

There is no universal cystometric capacity, BCI or residual threshold in this slide. The relevant endpoint is credible assessment of storage safety and emptying.

## Slide 10 | Consent: improvement and lifetime burden

### Clinical explanation

Define what the quoted outcome measures. AUSCO's zero-pad endpoint means participants reported no pad use at twelve months after activation. ContiClassic's device-survival endpoint concerns the implant remaining without the study-defined failure event. A patient can retain an implant and still leak; a revised device can ultimately provide excellent continence.

Consent should include residual SUI, persistent or new urgency, retention, infection, erosion, mechanical failure and future revision. Explain the consequence of explantation and how future catheterisation/endoscopy will require precautions.

### Evidence meaning

AUSCO26 was a prospective single-arm AMS 800 study of 115 implants, in which 61/101 evaluable participants reported zero pads (60.4%). Different endpoints had different denominators. CCSAFETY was an uncontrolled early ContiClassic report of 116 recipients, with 93.2% twelve-month Kaplan-Meier survival. The accessible abstract did not establish detailed continence outcomes. AUA24 supports lifetime counselling; RT22 adds observational radiation-associated risk. These data cannot rank devices against one another.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**FRAIL26:** In 7,252 men undergoing AUS or sling, claims-defined frailty was associated with 30-day complications independently of age and comorbidity. Relative to not frail, adjusted relative risks were 1.5 for prefrail and 2.5 for mildly-to-severely frail men. Revision/removal was also associated with frailty. The data mix AUS and sling procedures, come from historical Medicare records and rely on a claims frailty index. These relative risks are not an individual AUS-specific absolute probability, and they do not validate a bedside pump score. Use a practical frailty assessment alongside observed dexterity and cognition.

**REGRET26:** Ninety-one men responded (87.5%), with median follow-up 82 months. Reoperation was the only independent predictor of regret in the reported model (+18 points on the Decisional Regret Scale). Continence benefit persisted but deteriorated over time. This was a retrospective single-centre cohort of survivors who responded, with no nonsurgical comparator. Association does not prove reoperation causes all regret or predict an individual response. Counselling should include residual leakage, future surgery and psychological burden. Online December 2025; 2026 issue.

**DEVICE26:** The contemporary review describes new hydraulic and electronic designs, while emphasising the lack of robust direct comparisons and long-term outcomes for many devices. It supports keeping engineering advantages separate from proven clinical superiority. A review combining clinical, regulatory, manufacturer and preclinical materials is context rather than a head-to-head trial. Early ContiClassic safety cannot be compared with AMS 800 continence as if the endpoints were interchangeable.

**REPORT26:** A retrospective 227-implant series using systematic complication capture reported 47% early complications, predominantly minor, and 7.9% major complications requiring intervention. Five-year explantation-free survival was 73%. Selected techniques included distal double cuffs and transcorporal cuffs. These results are not a universal primary single-bulbar-cuff complication rate. Systematic reporting and endpoint definitions explain why superficially similar published percentages differ.

### A speaking line

"These percentages answer different questions. I can counsel meaningful improvement and likely future maintenance without promising permanent dryness."

### Be ready for

## Slide 12 | Urgency treatment and emptying

### Clinical explanation

Address fluid/caffeine patterns, constipation, infection when suspected and contributing medication. Choose bladder training and medication with comorbidity, cognition, interactions and residual urine in mind. An antimuscarinic may add cognitive burden and affect emptying; beta-3 agonists have their own contraindications and monitoring needs.

Before botulinum toxin, discuss possible retention, catheter competence and safe repeated cystoscopic access through a future cuff. Improving storage can create an emptying problem. Neuromodulation is another appropriate OAB-pathway option, depending on the patient. After AUS, reassess persistent urgency rather than automatically blaming the implant or simply increasing medication.

### Evidence meaning

AUAOAB24 supplies evidence-based OAB treatment choices and shared decision-making. BAUS25 situates mixed symptoms within the continence pathway. OAB11 shows that storage symptoms often persist despite improved SUI. CCIFU supplies device-specific bladder precautions. None establishes one universally superior sequence of medication, botulinum toxin and AUS, or a single AUS-specific prescription.

### A speaking line

"Stress leakage and urgency need separate treatment goals. Before escalating bladder treatment, I want a credible emptying and future-access plan."

The AUS temporarily opens for voiding, which makes its physiology different from a permanently fixed compressive outlet. Nevertheless, a man who relies on abdominal straining may not empty equally well after implantation. "I would investigate before deciding. The issue is whether he can empty reliably with the cuff opened and manage a realistic rescue strategy."

## Slide 14 | Catheterisation with a cuff in place

### Clinical explanation

Clarify why catheterisation is needed and how often. Recurrent narrowing is a different problem from occasional retention or chronic DU. Check actual competence before implantation, including access and recognising resistance. A plan may need a specialist alternative drainage route if repeated or prolonged urethral access is likely.

Before passing an instrument, fully deflate the cuff and deactivate the actual system. Use appropriate atraumatic instrumentation and stop with unexplained resistance. The nominal cuff size does not equal the free internal lumen: urethral tissue lies within it. A single French-size rule cannot replace individual/device assessment.

### Evidence meaning

CIC23 described 57 selected patients with a CIC history; only 18 continued or started CIC after AUS. Non-significant differences do not demonstrate equal safety. CATH13 associated catheterisation lasting more than 48 hours with erosion in an observational cohort. That exposure definition is not a guarantee of safety up to 48 hours. CCIFU and ICS16 provide instrumentation precautions and device-aware drainage principles.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**BAUSSCI26:** A small retrospective SCI series explored predominantly bulbar placement. It is a specialist discussion point. The cohort was small and selected, with no direct bladder-neck comparator and no proof of equivalence. Keep safe storage and an emptying plan central.

**INSTR26:** A survey of 286 Turkish urologists identified training and confidence gaps in AUS deactivation and instrumentation. It reinforces the practical value of patient/device information and a clear urgent-contact route. It was a self-selected knowledge survey and did not test catheter safety. Its proposed diameter/duration answers cannot become universal rules for every cuff, tissue or device. Follow the actual system's instructions.

### A speaking line

"The patient and any future clinician must know there is a cuff. Deflation and deactivation come before instrumentation, with the drainage route planned in advance."

### Be ready for

Careful CIC and prolonged indwelling catheterisation are different exposures; do not attribute identical risk to them.

## Slide 16 | Three different sites of narrowing

### Clinical explanation

Name the anatomy precisely. An anterior stricture involves urethra/spongiosum. Bladder-neck contracture typically follows prostate surgery with tissue remaining. After radical prostatectomy, narrowing at the bladder-to-urethra join is VUAS. Different labels imply different imaging, access, reconstructive operations and risk to continence.

Review prior incisions, dilations and repairs, including how quickly the lumen narrowed again. A single successful passage of a scope does not establish stability. Look for a convincing interval without recurrence supported by symptoms, flow, residual and appropriate endoscopic assessment. Plan future tumour surveillance or treatment of radiation bleeding before a cuff complicates access.

### Evidence meaning

EAUSTR26 supplies site-specific narrowing/reconstruction pathways. BAUS25 and AUA24 require relevant outlet pathology to be assessed and treated before continence surgery. Combining anatomical patency with clinical durability is practical synthesis. The teaching image illustrates general relationships and cannot define a patient's stricture length or reconstructive plane.

### A speaking line

"I want the exact site, prior treatment and recurrence pattern, because a temporarily open lumen may still be an unstable outlet."

### Be ready for

## Slide 17 | Reassessment and urethral stability

### Clinical explanation

The intervals refer to different pathways. AUA discusses cystoscopy at least four to six weeks after symptomatic BNC/VUAS treatment to document improvement/stability. EAU's three-to-six-month advice is tied to redo vesicourethral anastomosis. BAUS recommends at least six months without recurrence after treatment of post-prostatectomy narrowing.

Each interval is an opportunity to establish a durable outlet, and none is a guarantee set by the calendar. Frequent restenosis, irradiated tissue and continuing endoscopic treatment can remain decisive after an interval has elapsed. Staging also avoids repeated treatment through a newly cuffed urethra.

### Evidence meaning

AUA24, EAUSTR26 and BAUS25 provide the actual source-specific intervals; ICS16 gives broader consensus context. They rely largely on consensus and observational experience. No randomised timing trial establishes one uniquely safe waiting period for all repairs. AUA's earlier reassessment must not be quoted as approval for immediate implantation in every reconstructed urethra.

### A speaking line

"These numbers describe different clinical situations. I need evidence of stable patency and a realistic future-access plan, alongside the relevant guideline interval."

### Be ready for

If challenged about a shorter interval, explain the specific reconstruction and recurrence history before defending a number.

## Slide 18 | Case 3: radiation and recurrent VUAS

### Clinical explanation

This fictional patient has severe SUI when the outlet is open, but patency depends on repeated endoscopic intervention after prostatectomy and salvage radiation. Implanting now would place a cuff within an ongoing access/restenosis problem. Assess storage function and radiation cystitis as well as the lumen.

In a devastated outlet with unsafe or intolerable bladder function, a different drainage/diversion endpoint may offer better quality of life. ### Evidence meaning

These pooled associations do not give his individual probability or prove that radiation alone contraindicates AUS. BAUS25, EAUSTR26 and AUA24 supply outlet stability and reconstructive guidance. ### Current evidence and 2026 update

**RTTIME25:** In 283 men, prior radiation was associated with cuff removal (HR 3.30, 95% CI 1.05-10.40). Exploratory modelling suggested a later-implantation trend after radiation, but this was not statistically significant. The wide interval and retrospective spline analysis do not validate a 635-day or 1.74-year waiting rule. Radiation risk, oncological timing, urethral stability and individual function must be considered separately.

### A speaking line

"I would defer the implant while we agree a durable reconstructive endpoint. Radiation adds risk, but the immediate problem is recurrent stenosis and likely further access."

### Be ready for

Do not convert an odds ratio into an absolute patient risk. Explain what extra information would allow reconsideration: durable patency, acceptable bladder function and a manageable access plan.

## Slide 19 | AUS after urethral reconstruction

### Clinical explanation

Prior urethroplasty is not a single exposure. Identify the repair site, whether it transected the urethra, grafts, posterior reconstruction, fistula history and previous cuff erosion. Retrieve operative reports to understand remaining blood supply and viable cuff-bearing tissue.

Limit mobilisation and preserve remaining attachments rather than reproducing virgin-urethra dissection. A convenient circumference does not prove healthy tissue. An unsafe plane can justify an alternative approach, staging or seeking reconstructive expertise. Earlier reconstructive choices and later continence surgery are connected decisions.

### Evidence meaning

Case selection, anatomy, disease severity and follow-up can confound that comparison. BAUS25, AUA24 and ICS16 provide the clinical framework. The cohort supports detailed counselling/history, but does not prove every transection caused failure or was avoidable.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**URETHMETA26:** Four studies included 533 men. Prior urethroplasty was associated with higher explantation risk (RR 2.05, 95% CI 1.08-3.89); its pooled erosion difference was not statistically significant. Within repair-technique comparisons, transecting versus non-transecting repair was associated with higher erosion (RR 2.34, 1.30-4.21) and explantation (RR 2.38, 1.59-3.57). ### A speaking line

"I need the reconstruction history in detail because it changes the tissue available for the cuff and the safety of the next dissection."

### Be ready for

## Slide 20 | Transcorporal placement: role and costs

### Clinical explanation

Transcorporal placement can incorporate tunical tissue under the cuff and avoid a hazardous dorsal urethral plane. Its rationale should address a particular anatomical problem. It also involves corporal dissection, bleeding risk, erectile consequences and potential implications for penile prosthesis surgery.

Comparisons are difficult because surgeons often reserve this approach for the most compromised urethras. Worse outcomes can reflect case severity; apparently favourable outcomes can reflect different selection or follow-up. Tandem cuffs and other salvage strategies also add complexity or consume tissue, and need an explicit indication.

### Evidence meaning

TC20's 76-patient fragile-urethra retrospective series favoured transcorporal revision outcomes; its erosion difference was not statistically significant. TC23's different high-risk series associated transcorporal placement with more infection/erosion-related explants. BAUS25 and AP23 recognise specialist salvage choices. Conflicting observational studies do not establish universal protection from erosion or prove that transcorporal placement itself causes the worse result.

### Current evidence and 2026 update

**TCSYS25:** Twenty studies were included, eight comparative. The meta-analysis associated transcorporal placement with more revisions (OR 2.99, 95% CI 1.16-7.75), less erosion (OR 0.35, 0.15-0.81) and infection (OR 0.33, 0.12-0.95), with no significant explantation difference. The series were non-randomised with heterogeneous risk selection, and the pooled estimates conflict with some individual cohorts. Search ended January 2024 despite online publication in November 2025. Present it as a specialist option with uncertain causal protection, not proof that every fragile urethra should receive it.

### A speaking line

"I would choose transcorporal placement for a defined dissection problem, while explaining its costs and the uncertainty about comparative erosion protection."

### Be ready for

Confounding by indication means the treatment was selected because the patient was already at higher risk. It is a major limitation of these series.

## Slide 21 | The fifteen-minute wash question

## Slide 27 | Bulbar dissection and tissue preservation

### Clinical explanation

## Slide 28 | Cuff measurement and fit

### Clinical explanation

Measure the tissue the cuff will actually surround. Retraction stretches the urethra; a tight measuring tape compresses it. Included scar, fat or muscle, oedema and haematoma can enlarge or distort the apparent circumference. Release traction, confirm the enclosed tissue and use the system's supplied method.

A small cuff can produce excessive compression, retention or injury; a large cuff may leave inadequate coaptation. Higher pressure cannot reliably rescue a sizing error. In transcorporal or reconstructed anatomy, the enclosed tissue differs, so an unsupported numerical correction is inappropriate. Recheck a reading that does not fit the operative appearance.

### Evidence meaning

ICS16 and AP23 provide AMS 800 consensus sizing principles; CCIFU governs the actual ContiClassic system. CCSAFETY reported four revisions attributed to mistaken sizing among its eight revisions. That illustrates a clinically important error, but does not identify a superior sizing method or prove that more cuff-size choices improve survival.

### A speaking line

"A good measurement comes from undistorted tissue and the correct product method. A stronger balloon does not compensate for a poor fit."

### Be ready for

Cuff circumference in centimetres is not a catheter lumen size. Distinguish size selection from pressure selection.

## Slide 29 | Urethral injury or contamination

## Slide 34 | Aftercare, activation and urgent review

### Clinical explanation

### Evidence meaning

## Slide 41 | Comparative and preparation evidence

### Clinical explanation

This table separates a randomised clinical comparison, a skin-culture surrogate trial and a sequential-era infection study. MASTER 2026 is the 24-month follow-up of the same 380 men randomised in MASTER 2022; it is not an independent second trial. The sling-minus-AUS success difference at 24 months was -0.6 percentage points (95% CI -9.2 to +8.0). The interval remained above the prespecified -15-point non-inferiority margin. This supports the trial's primary conclusion in its selected population, without establishing identical outcomes or general equivalence.

### Evidence meaning

MASTER22 and MASTER26 should be read together. YEUNG13 found 4/50 versus 16/50 positive post-preparation skin cultures (8% versus 32%) in mixed genitourinary prosthetic procedures; it did not test clinical AUS infection or a specific preparation duration.

Only three events, wide confidence intervals and sequential-era confounding prevent an equivalence conclusion. Broad intravenous and ten-day oral prophylaxis continued, as did other antiseptic measures. The exposure combined device immersion and wound irrigation, so their separate effects cannot be inferred. It does not establish a ContiClassic preparation protocol.

PREP24 remains in the source dictionary as an earlier preparation study, with its reporting limitations; it is no longer the third row of this updated backup table.

### A speaking line

"MASTER's 24-month follow-up retained non-inferiority on its primary endpoint, while further surgery favoured AUS. A culture trial and a small sequential-era infection study answer different questions."

### Be ready for

Non-significance is not equivalence. Preserve denominators, the timepoint and the co-interventions when discussing preparation studies. No single study settles local antibiotic policy or device-specific instructions.

## Slide 42 | Evidence gaps

### Clinical explanation

Each gap needs a defined population, intervention and meaningful outcome. Routine versus selective UDS should measure management change, safety, patient outcomes and burden. DU studies should distinguish reliable emptying from unresolved retention, and report early complications rather than exclude them.

Reconstruction studies need repair type, radiation, recurrence and future-access details. Salvage comparisons need comparable baseline urethral risk. Preparation/antibiotic research should measure clinical infection or explantation and include adverse effects, rather than rely solely on skin cultures. New-device studies need standard continence definitions, revision indications, subgroup data and long follow-up.

### Evidence meaning

The slide synthesises limitations across the cited literature and introduces no new trial result. These are unanswered comparative questions, not proof that the competing practices have equal outcomes or permission to bypass established precautions.

### A speaking line

"The useful research question specifies which patients, which intervention and which outcome would change care. We should avoid using heterogeneous endpoints to claim certainty."

### Be ready for

For late leakage, standardised investigation and failure definitions matter as much as comparing revision operations: the cause assigned to failure can depend on how thoroughly the device was investigated.

**Limits:** The programme and title were verified, but complete methods, results and denominators were unavailable. No numerical result or change in practice is inferred. This is conference material, not an AUA guideline.

**Source and access:** [AUA 2026 IP09-17: SAFER erosion management](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.JU.0001191316.72449.22.17). AUA 2026 IP09-17: SAFER erosion management. Official journal/programme record and publisher-deposited Crossref metadata only. First available: 2026.

### MASTER26 | MASTER: 24-month RCT results, 2026

This is extended follow-up of the same 380 randomised men, not a second independent trial. At 24 months, sling met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion on the strict self-reported continence endpoint: risk difference -0.006 (95% CI -0.092 to 0.080), or -0.6 percentage points (-9.2 to +8.0). Further surgery was reported in 20 men (11%) after sling and four (2%) after AUS. Secondary outcomes generally favoured AUS.

**Limits:** The 15-percentage-point margin applies to the prespecified endpoint, not every outcome. Do not translate non-inferiority into identical effectiveness, zero-pad equivalence or an endorsement for irradiated/reconstructed revision cases. Abstract symptom-score means and adjusted difference are distinct estimates; do not subtract them and label it the adjusted effect.

**Source and access:** [The 24-mo Outcomes from the Noninferiority Randomised Controlled Trial of Surgery for Men with Urodynamic Stress Incontinence After Prostate Surgery (MASTER).](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euf.2026.07.012). Constable L, Drake MJ, Cooper D, MacLennan G, Harding C, Mundy A, McCormack K, Norrie J, Ramsay C, Smith R, Cotterill N, Kilonzo M, Abrams P, MASTER trial team. | European urology focus 2026 | DOI 10.1016/j.euf.2026.07.012. Publisher abstract via Europe PMC. First available: 2026-08-26.

### URETHMETA26 | Prior urethroplasty: meta-analysis, 2026

Four studies included 533 men. Prior urethroplasty was associated with higher explantation risk (RR 2.05, 95% CI 1.08-3.89); its pooled erosion difference was not statistically significant. Within repair-technique comparisons, transecting versus non-transecting repair was associated with higher erosion (RR 2.34, 1.30-4.21) and explantation (RR 2.38, 1.59-3.57).

**Limits:** These are separate comparisons. Pooled observational studies, some overlapping with Davis, do not make the association causal or independently validate one reconstructive strategy. Explain tissue quality and individual anatomy.

**Source and access:** [Association of Prior Urethroplasty and Technique With Outcomes After Artificial Urinary Sphincter Implantation: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urology.2026.05.024). Deameh MG, Mohamed H, Abdelshafi A, Mohamed T, Ramez M. | Urology 2026 | DOI 10.1016/j.urology.2026.05.024. Publisher abstract via Europe PMC. First available: 2026-06-04.

### IRRIG26 | Gentamicin withdrawal: AMS 800 cohort, 2026

The before-after study included 173 uncoated AMS 800 procedures in 164 men: 59 gentamicin-containing and 114 gentamicin-free. Ninety-day SSI occurred in 1/59 (1.7%) and 2/114 (1.8%), respectively; the risk difference was +0.1 percentage points (95% CI -7.4 to +4.7). No significant difference was detected.

**Limits:** Only three SSIs occurred. The interval allows clinically relevant benefit or harm, so this is not equivalence. Device immersion and wound irrigation changed together; saline immersion and Betadine-containing irrigation continued. Broad IV prophylaxis and ten-day oral antibiotics continued, with an oral-drug change in the later era. It cannot establish that no preparation is needed, that oral prophylaxis is unnecessary, or that the same result applies to coated AMS 800 or ContiClassic.

**Source and access:** [Early Outcomes After Discontinuing Routine Gentamicin Irrigation During AMS 800 Artificial Urinary Sphincter Implantation: A Single-Centre Retrospective Before-After Cohort Study.](https://doi.org/10.3390/antibiotics15090894). Skrzypczyk MA, Pliszka A, Białek Ł, Rydzińska M, Karoń W, Dobruch J, Lemiński A. | Antibiotics (Basel, Switzerland) 2026 | DOI 10.3390/antibiotics15090894. Full open article read. First available: 2026-09-11.

### DAYMETA26 | Day-case AUS meta-analysis: 2026 issue

Six studies included 2,424 men; four (1,085) entered the meta-analysis. No statistically significant difference was detected in retention (RR 1.44, 95% CI 0.81-2.56) or 90-day emergency visits (RR 0.91, 0.59-1.42).

**Limits:** Moderate-to-serious bias and wide intervals prevent equivalence claims. Same-day catheter-free discharge is a selected pathway, not a universal instruction. Online August 2025; March 2026 issue.

**Source and access:** [Same-day catheter-free discharge versus overnight observation after artificial urinary sphincter surgery: a systematic review and meta-analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11255-025-04711-y). ### DAYCASE26 | Day-case versus inpatient AUS: 177 men, 2026

A retrospective primary AMS 800 comparison included 126 ambulatory and 51 inpatient men. The evaluable 30-day composite was 14/124 (11.3%) versus 4/49 (8.2%), p=0.78.

**Limits:** Inpatient selection depended on ASA score and social circumstances. Confounding and different enrolled/evaluable denominators matter. The abstract's early/severe complication percentages are not sufficiently clear for additional teaching statistics. A nonsignificant result is not proof of equal safety.

**Source and access:** [Day-case versus conventional inpatient artificial urinary sphincter implantation: A single-center comparative study of 177 patients.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fjurol.2026.103135). Youssef MW, Perrin L, Denormandie AC, Chartier-Kastler E, Lenfant L. | The French journal of urology 2026 | DOI 10.1016/j.fjurol.2026.103135. Publisher abstract via Europe PMC. First available: 2026-05-29.

### FRAIL26 | Frailty and SUI surgery: Medicare cohort, 2026

In 7,252 men undergoing AUS or sling, claims-defined frailty was associated with 30-day complications independently of age and comorbidity. Relative to not frail, adjusted relative risks were 1.5 for prefrail and 2.5 for mildly-to-severely frail men. Revision/removal was also associated with frailty.

**Limits:** The data mix AUS and sling procedures, come from historical Medicare records and rely on a claims frailty index. These relative risks are not an individual AUS-specific absolute probability, and they do not validate a bedside pump score. Use a practical frailty assessment alongside observed dexterity and cognition.

**Source and access:** [Frailty Is Associated With Postoperative Complications and Device Removal and Revision in Men Undergoing Surgery for Stress Urinary Incontinence.](https://doi.org/10.1097/ju.0000000000005102). Nik-Ahd F, Dreyfuss LD, Wang L, Shatkin-Margolis A, Boscardin WJ, Hampson LA, Breyer BN, Covinsky K, Suskind AM. | The Journal of urology 2026 | DOI 10.1097/ju.0000000000005102. Publisher abstract via Europe PMC. First available: 2026-07-15.

### PAD26 | Pad count versus 24-hour weight: 2026

A retrospective analysis of prospectively collected follow-up included 2,040 men and 8,787 paired measurements. Pad count tracked leakage well overall (Spearman rho 0.94), but two-to-four pads/day had considerable weight variability.

**Limits:** Repeated observations are not independent additional patients; single-centre first-year post-RP follow-up is not a universal AUS selection rule. Pad weight is particularly helpful when the intermediate count, activity or pad type leaves severity uncertain.

**Source and access:** [The 24-h pad test in the assessment of post-prostatectomy incontinence: is there still a role for counting pads per day?](https://doi.org/10.1111/bju.70224). ### PRBFAT26 | PRB material fatigue: 2026 issue

Mechanical testing compared 32 retrieved balloons with one new balloon; seven underwent additional testing. Age correlated with increased stiffness and reduced wall thickness; microcracks were seen in the four oldest specimens tested microscopically.

**Limits:** Retrieved components are selected samples; laboratory ageing is mechanistic evidence, not a trial of balloon-only versus complete-system revision. Do not replace a balloon solely because three years have elapsed. Online December 2025; 2026 issue.

**Source and access:** [Time Dependent Material Fatigue of the Artificial Urinary Sphincter Pressure-regulating Balloon: A Mechanical and Microscopic Analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urology.2025.12.031). ### REGRET26 | Long-term decisional regret after AUS, 2026 issue

Ninety-one men responded (87.5%), with median follow-up 82 months. Reoperation was the only independent predictor of regret in the reported model (+18 points on the Decisional Regret Scale). Continence benefit persisted but deteriorated over time.

**Limits:** This was a retrospective single-centre cohort of survivors who responded, with no nonsurgical comparator. Association does not prove reoperation causes all regret or predict an individual response. Counselling should include residual leakage, future surgery and psychological burden. Online December 2025; 2026 issue.

### INSTR26 | AUS instrumentation knowledge survey, 2026

A survey of 286 Turkish urologists identified training and confidence gaps in AUS deactivation and instrumentation. It reinforces the practical value of patient/device information and a clear urgent-contact route.

### EROSION26 | Immediate urethral repair at explantation, 2026

In a selected single-centre series of 16 men, one developed a stricture after repair at eroded-cuff explantation (6.3%); seven developed wound dehiscence requiring closure (43.8%). The low stricture rate therefore sits beside a substantial wound-treatment burden.

## Rehearsal prompts and evidence language

- **What contraindicates implantation?** Distinguish unresolved infection, unsafe storage, inability to empty/use the pump and unstable narrowing. DO alone is different; the actual device label may be more restrictive.
- **Which rate do you quote?** State the device, population, endpoint, time point and denominator. Prefer a comparable cohort; do not present an early survival percentage as a continence rate.
- **Does a non-significant result show no difference?** No. It may reflect limited power or imprecision. Equivalence and non-inferiority need prespecified margins and appropriate analyses.
- **What does a 95% CI tell you?** It describes the range of estimates compatible with the data/model at that confidence level. A wide interval signals uncertainty; inclusion of the no-effect value does not prove equal outcomes.
- **What is a hazard ratio?** A relative time-to-event rate under a model, not the absolute probability of an event. The definition of event, coding/reference group and time assumptions must be checked.
- **Why avoid a universal cuff/catheter rule?** Cuff circumference, enclosed tissue and available lumen are different measures. Device state, patient anatomy and current instructions matter.
- **What changes your revision plan?** Whether leakage reflects use, urethra, mechanics or bladder. Investigate before assuming atrophy or increasing compression.
