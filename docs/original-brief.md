### Required content

**1. Patient selection and counselling**

Cover:
- Establishing sphincteric stress incontinence and identifying mixed symptoms.
- Symptom burden, pad use and pad weight, patient goals, and realistic expectations.
- Timing after prostate treatment and conservative management.
- AUS versus sling or other appropriate options, without allowing this comparison to dominate.
- Prior radiotherapy, salvage treatment, previous continence surgery, and previous urethral reconstruction.
- Manual dexterity, cognition, pump accessibility, frailty, and the patient’s ability to operate the device over time.
- Counselling about persistent urgency, residual leakage, revision, erosion, infection, mechanical failure, and future instrumentation.
- When to proceed, optimise, defer, or advise against implantation.

**2. Flexible cystoscopy and urodynamics**

Treat “flexion” as flexible cystoscopy.

Answer explicitly:
- Who should undergo preoperative flexible cystoscopy, when, and why?
- What findings change the operative plan?
- How should suspected stricture, bladder-neck contracture, or vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis be investigated?
- When is urodynamic testing necessary, useful, or unlikely to change management?
- Do BAUS, EAU, and AUA/GURS/SUFU differ on routine versus selective testing?
- How should severe leakage, low cystometric capacity, equivocal contractility, or technically limited studies affect interpretation?
- Which findings change counselling alone, and which change treatment or timing?

Provide a practical investigation pathway in text.

**3. Detrusor overactivity, detrusor underactivity, and impaired compliance**

Separate these entities clearly.

For detrusor overactivity:
- Distinguish urodynamic findings from symptomatic urgency and urgency incontinence.
- Discuss when to treat before implantation and when implantation may still be appropriate.
- Explain counselling about persistent storage symptoms.
- Discuss relevant treatment sequencing, including retention and future instrumentation implications.

For detrusor underactivity:
- Explain how to assess emptying, residual urine, abdominal straining, and retention risk.
- Address whether underactivity necessarily excludes AUS.
- Discuss patients who may require intermittent catheterisation and how this changes planning and counselling.
- Avoid inventing a universal contractility or residual-volume cutoff.

For poor compliance:
- Explain why it requires separate assessment from detrusor overactivity.
- Address storage pressures, upper-tract concerns, optimisation, and reassessment before increasing outlet resistance.

Use brief cases and an “implant now / optimise first / investigate further / reconsider AUS” framework, with evidence and uncertainty clearly stated.

**4. Strictures, stenoses, and the compromised urethra**

Cover:
- Previous urethral stricture, bladder-neck contracture, and vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis as distinct problems.
- Active or recurrent disease versus a previously treated stable narrowing.
- Previous dilation, urethrotomy, urethroplasty, radiation, erosion, and explantation.
- Staged versus synchronous management where relevant.
- How urethral stability is established and whether proposed waiting periods are evidence-based or expert practice.
- Anticipated future endoscopic access.
- How prior reconstruction affects dissection, cuff siting, and erosion risk.
- Selected roles and limitations of transcorporal placement and other approaches for a compromised urethra.

Do not present controversial salvage techniques as routine best practice.

**5. Theatre preparation: evidence versus ritual**

Explicitly answer:

**“Is a 15-minute wash or scrub before AUS implantation necessary, and what evidence supports it?”**

First clarify the difference between:
- Patient preoperative washing.
- Operative-site skin preparation and the product’s required contact and drying times.
- Surgical hand antisepsis.
- Intraoperative wound or device irrigation.

Then critically examine:
- Whether there is direct AUS-specific evidence for a fixed 15-minute preparation.
- Whether evidence is extrapolated from penile prosthesis surgery or general implant surgery.
- Antiseptic choice, anatomical suitability, contraindications, and manufacturer instructions.
- Hair removal, urine testing and management of relevant findings.
- Antibiotic prophylaxis, timing, redosing, and postoperative antibiotics.
- Draping, device handling, glove changes, traffic, and contamination reduction.
- Irrigation and device preparation, distinguishing evidence from tradition and device-specific instructions.

Avoid an unsupported universal antibiotic or antiseptic recipe. End with a practical theatre checklist, identifying what is supported, what depends on local policy or the device, and what remains uncertain.

**6. Operative tips and tricks**

This should be a central section. Explain the problem each manoeuvre addresses, its limitations, and how it can cause harm if used poorly.

Cover:
- Positioning, exposure, and approach selection.
- Safe bulbar urethral dissection and tissue preservation.
- Cuff site selection, measurement, sizing, and common errors.
- Avoiding urethral injury and recognising it intraoperatively.
- What to do if urethral injury or contamination occurs.
- Pressure-regulating balloon placement in routine and previously operated pelvises.
- Device-specific pressure selection and preparation according to verified instructions.
- Pump positioning, accessibility, and avoiding migration or discomfort.
- Tubing routing, connections, kinking, device cycling, and deactivation.
- Catheter choice and duration.
- Decisions that should prompt stopping, changing the plan, or staging surgery.

Separate broadly applicable surgical principles from ContiClassic-specific steps. Do not substitute remembered AMS 800 instructions for verified ContiClassic instructions.
